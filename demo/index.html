Flagship

The Burpee Zone

12 burpee stations under one rig, 4 cameras in the corners. Every burpee counted and checked, for every athlete, at the same time.

Tap to enlarge

What it does

  • Knows who's there. The athlete's timing chip is scanned every time they enter the zone, so the AI knows exactly who is on which station.
  • Sees every station. 4 cameras high on the rig corners see all 12 stations from different sides, so one athlete standing in front of another doesn't break the count.
  • Counts to the standard. Chest to the ground, hips and knees extended, a jump with the hands above the ears. A burpee that misses any of these is a no-rep, with the reason.
  • Live everywhere. The count shows on the big screen, on referees' phones and on the YouTube stream as it happens.
  • Referees decide the close ones. A doubtful rep goes to the referee's phone with its clip: one tap to accept or reject.
  • Checked out and saved. The chip is scanned again at the exit; the athlete's count and clip are saved to their result.

  • The best moment, picked by the AI. The bell strike, the stone held high, the top of a burpee jump: sharp, face visible, from the 4K video.

  • Branded and ready to share. Framed in Spartan branding (and a sponsor's, if wanted): every share is Spartan marketing.
  • A library for Spartan. Action shots from every event and every obstacle, for social media, sponsors and the stream's highlights.
  • Could earn its keep. Free with the result, or a photo pack to buy.
  • Privacy first. Only for athletes who agree when they register; nobody else's photo is sent or shared.

How it works

The hardware, and where the pictures go

  1. 1 · At the obstacle

    4 outdoor 4K cameras on the rig corners, with microphones. The timing-chip readers at the entry and exit. For a single obstacle, one phone on a tripod running our Broadcaster app is enough.

  2. 2 · On site

    One small box on the rig: a network switch that also powers the cameras, and a 5G router (Starlink as a backup). It sends the video up. Nothing else to install.

  3. 3 · The AI judge, in the cloud

    A graphics-card server in Microsoft Azure, booked for the event. It tracks every body, counts the reps, spots the fails and matches each athlete to their chip.

  4. 4 · Out to people, live

    Referees' phones: live counts, one tap on a flagged rep. The big screen: the leaderboard. YouTube Live: the stream with the counts on it. Athletes' phones: their result and clip.

How we roll it out

  1. Today: the AI judge works

    It already counts burpees from our cameras. The World Championship recordings from Abu Dhabi were judged by it.

  2. Buy one kit, build one zone

    The cameras, switch, router and chip readers above, fixed to one Burpee Zone rig. Set it up and test it at our own site first.

  3. Pilot at a few events

    Run it at 2 or 3 events alongside our referees, who still make every call. We compare its counts with theirs and fix what it gets wrong.

  4. Then the obstacles

    Once the Burpee Zone is right: Atlas Carry, Chain Carry and Rope Climb, each with its own camera or two, set up as shown above.

  5. Go live

    The AI's counts become the result, with the referee able to overrule. The leaderboard and YouTube stream go public, and more kits follow for more events.

Beyond refereeing

AI Possibilities

The cameras, the AI judge and the referee platform are the start. The same technology can do far more, for referees, athletes, fans and the teams who run the course.

01Built

AI Assistant Referee and learning platform

An expert referee in every referee's pocket

An app for every Spartan referee, to help them do the job better. Ask any rules question at the obstacle and get the answer in seconds, with the rule it comes from. Lessons, quick checks and refreshers keep every referee to the same standard, and they work with no signal.

ShotA referee at the Atlas Carry asks whether the athlete can put the stone down. The answer comes back with rule 4.1.3, and their Atlas Carry lesson ticks over underneath.

02Part built

VAR for obstacle racing

Every obstacle judged. Every call on video.

Every obstacle auto-judged: pass or fail, and any penalty, written straight to the athlete's timing record with the video clip attached for appeals. The referee platform becomes the human in the loop: referees review what the AI flags and always have the final say. The burpee judge is built; the Atlas Carry, Chain Carry and Rope Climb above are the next three examples.

ShotOne athlete's timing record fills in obstacle by obstacle. The Chain Carry is flagged: the referee watches the clip on their phone, taps Confirm, and the penalty and the clip are saved to the record.

03Possible

Personal highlight reels

Their race, their film, before they've caught their breath

The AI finds each athlete by their bib on every camera and edits a film of their race within minutes of the finish. A memento every athlete will want to keep, and to share.

ShotBib 2076 is found at four obstacles, the clips are cut together, and the finished film plays in Spartan branding, on the athlete's phone 4 minutes after the finish.

04Possible

AI-driven live video and audio commentary

Every elite heat, covered like a Grand Prix

A live broadcast of the elite heats with obstacle split times, live leaderboards, win predictions and skeleton overlays, and commentary written and voiced by the AI as it happens.

ShotElite heat 2 at the Rope Climb: the climber's skeleton is tracked, the bell brings up the split time, the leaderboard reshuffles, the win chances swing, and the AI commentator calls it.

05Possible

Spartan Score

A score for every athlete, and a reason to come back

Speed and technique at every obstacle, benchmarked against the elites, feeding a personal training plan after the race. It keeps athletes training, and engaged with Spartan, between races.

ShotAfter the race, the athlete's Spartan Score counts up, each obstacle is set against the elite benchmark, and a 4-week plan builds itself around the weakest.

06Mostly built

At-home burpee judge

Qualify for Spartan from your living room

The burpee judge we already have, on a phone camera: virtual qualifiers and global challenges, judged to the same standard as the Burpee Zone, for anyone, anywhere. It already runs in a phone's browser.

ShotA phone on the floor counts the burpees, rejects one with the chest off the ground, and the count climbs the global leaderboard live.

07Possible

Safety and course ops

An athlete down is seen in seconds, not minutes

The cameras watch for falls and athletes who don't get up, and alert the medics with the exact spot. They predict queues at obstacles too, so the course team can act before one builds.

ShotThe course from above: the AI predicts a queue at obstacle 7 ten minutes ahead; an athlete falls at obstacle 9 and stays down, and the nearest medic is sent straight there.

08Possible

3D course view

Walk the course before race day

Anyone can explore the racecourse in 3D before the event, with accurate gradients and landmarks. On race day, athletes' live positions move on the same 3D course for friends, family and fans.
