How it works

  • Live. The photo reaches the athlete's phone moments after the obstacle, while they're still on the course, and the best shots of the day go up on the big screen and the stream as they happen.
  • The best moment, picked by the AI. The bell strike, the stone held high, the top of a burpee jump: sharp, face visible, from the 4K video.
  • Matched by the timing chip. Each photo goes to the right athlete, with no photographer at every obstacle.
  • Branded and ready to share. Framed in Spartan branding (and a sponsor's, if wanted): every share is Spartan marketing.
  • A library for Spartan. Action shots from every event and every obstacle, for social media, sponsors and the stream's highlights.
  • Could earn its keep. Free with the result, or a photo pack to buy.
  • Privacy first. Only for athletes who agree when they register; nobody else's photo is sent or shared.

No extra hardware: the same cameras and AI judge as the refereeing.

Flagship

The Burpee Zone

12 burpee stations under one rig, 4 cameras in the corners. Every burpee counted and checked, for every athlete, at the same time.

Tap to enlarge

What it does

  • Knows who's there. The athlete's timing chip is scanned every time they enter the zone, so the AI knows exactly who is on which station.
  • Sees every station. 4 cameras high on the rig corners see all 12 stations from different sides, so one athlete standing in front of another doesn't break the count.
  • Counts to the standard. Chest to the ground, hips and knees extended, a jump with the hands above the ears. A burpee that misses any of these is a no-rep, with the reason.
  • Live everywhere. The count shows on the big screen, on referees' phones and on the YouTube stream as it happens.
  • Referees decide the close ones. A doubtful rep goes to the referee's phone with its clip: one tap to accept or reject.
  • Checked out and saved. The chip is scanned again at the exit; the athlete's count and clip are saved to their result.

Obstacle · Rule 4.1.3

Atlas Carry

  • Confirms the stone is lifted clear of the ground and carried, never rolled.
  • Confirms it went past the far flags and was placed back at the start.
  • If it's put down, pins the exact spot and checks it's picked up from there.

Obstacle

Chain Carry

  • Tracks the athlete round the full loop, checkpoint by checkpoint.
  • Flags a chain that is dropped or dragged.
  • Flags course cutting.

Obstacle

Rope Climb

  • Confirms the bell strike two ways: seen by the camera and heard by the microphone.
  • Times the climb, from feet leaving the ground to the bell.
  • Classifies the technique (foot lock, S-wrap, arms only).

How it works

The hardware, and where the pictures go

  1. 1 · At the obstacle

    4 outdoor 4K cameras on the rig corners, with microphones. The timing-chip readers at the entry and exit. For a single obstacle, one phone on a tripod running our Broadcaster app is enough.

  2. 2 · On site

    One small box on the rig: a network switch that also powers the cameras, and a 5G router (Starlink as a backup). It sends the video up. Nothing else to install.

  3. 3 · The AI judge, in the cloud

    A graphics-card server in Microsoft Azure, booked for the event. It tracks every body, counts the reps, spots the fails and matches each athlete to their chip.

  4. 4 · Out to people, live

    Referees' phones: live counts, one tap on a flagged rep. The big screen: the leaderboard. YouTube Live: the stream with the counts on it. Athletes' phones: their result and clip.

How we roll it out

  1. Today: the AI judge works

    It already counts burpees from our cameras. The World Championship recordings from Abu Dhabi were judged by it.

  2. Buy one kit, build one zone

    The cameras, switch, router and chip readers above, fixed to one Burpee Zone rig. Set it up and test it at our own site first.

  3. Pilot at a few events

    Run it at 2 or 3 events alongside our referees, who still make every call. We compare its counts with theirs and fix what it gets wrong.

  4. Add the obstacles

    Atlas Carry, Chain Carry and Rope Climb, each with its own camera or phone, at the same pilot events.

  5. Go live

    The AI's counts become the result, with the referee able to overrule. The leaderboard and YouTube stream go public, and more kits follow for more events.
